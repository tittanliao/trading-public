S1 V3.9 Bollinger %B at entry — the strongest effect in the programme, and why it is not a filter

Bollinger %B at entry clears every screen in the programme — and filtering on it still loses money.

HomeXAUUSDTXWhat Didn’t WorkJargon

What was measured

S1 V3.9's entry Bollinger %B shows a monotone win-rate gradient topping at 73.17% for entries above the upper band against a 55.93% baseline. Does it survive the screens that reduced every other candidate in this programme to an artefact, and if it does, is filtering on it worth anything after opportunity cost?

Findings

The gradient is real, and it is the first thing in this programme to clear every screen

Entries above the upper band win 73.17% on 82 trades against a 55.93% baseline — a 20.86-point gap where the smallest gap these samples could resolve is 16.9. Family permutation across all seven buckets gives p=0.003 with an observed max |t| of 3.14 against a null median of 1.37. A moving-block bootstrap puts the 90% interval at 10.96 to 29.64 points, excluding zero in every one of 4000 resamples. Nothing else in twenty-four studies has done this.

It strengthens out of sample while the strategy itself decays

Above-upper win rate runs 65.12% on train (n=43), 81.25% on validation (n=16) and 82.61% on holdout (n=23). Over the same three periods the baseline goes 56.76%, 57.63%, 51.58% — the strategy got worse and this bucket got better. That is the opposite of the overfitting signature: RS-XAUUSD-20260819-001's slot basket ran Sharpe 3.07 to -2.12 across the same kind of split.

The response is continuous, so it is not a bucketing artefact

Sorted into %B deciles the win rate rises from 36.17% in the lowest to 81.25% in the highest, Spearman 0.839. It also keeps rising *inside* the above-upper bucket: the lower half of those 82 trades wins 65.85% and the upper half 80.49%. A real gradient does that; an effect manufactured by where a boundary was drawn shows a step at the boundary and flatness either side of it.

It survives the momentum it could have been a proxy for

Closing outside the band is itself a strong move, so the obvious deflation is that the band is measuring momentum. Split by six-bar entry momentum, the above-upper advantage holds in all three terciles: +8.8 points in the lowest, +14.21 in the middle, +21.93 in the highest. It does grow with momentum, so the two are not independent — but the band adds something at every level of it rather than only where momentum is already extreme.

Filtering on it destroys more return than it saves, and the two objectives point at different thresholds

Requiring %B above 1.0 lifts win rate to 73.17% and profit factor to 4.541 — while keeping 17.4% of the entries and 44.0% of the total return. Push it to 1.1 and win rate reaches 79.59% on 10.4% of trades and 30.6% of the return. The threshold that maximises total return is 0.4, which filters almost nothing (94.9% kept) for +4.4% return, itself well inside noise. Best win rate and best return do not agree at any threshold tested.

This is the same lesson RS-XAUUSD-20260818-001 already paid for

That study lifted S1 to 88.6% win rate with a take-profit overlay and cut return from +88.6% to between +14% and +36%. Here the mechanism is different — discarding entries rather than truncating winners — and the arithmetic lands in the same place. A rule that raises win rate by taking fewer trades has to be judged in return, and when it is, this one loses.

The prior table understated it because it saw a third of the trades

RS-XAUUSD-20260727-001 joined %B for 165 of its 450 trades and reported above_upper at 79.31% on n=29. On the full 472 with complete coverage the figure is 73.17% on n=82 — lower, and far better evidenced. Its neighbouring buckets moved too: near_middle went from 28.0% on n=25 to 40.74% on n=81. The direction survived triple the sample; the extremes were small-sample noise.

An unprompted impression, the Pine tooltip and the data agree, and that is why it was worth testing

An unprompted impression that signals high in the band win more often. V3.9's Pine already carries a disabled use_bb_filter whose tooltip records a 77.8% win rate for entries above the upper band. Neither is evidence — a tooltip is not a study and an impression is not a sample — but three independent statements of the same claim justified spending the screens on it rather than dismissing it with the others.

Its value is as information at decision time, not as a gate

Everything above points the same way: %B genuinely separates outcomes, and acting on it by refusing entries costs money. That is precisely the shape of a number that belongs in front of a human who is deciding, and does not belong in the strategy. It is already displayed that way in the signal-feedback message, with its sample size attached and no recommendation.

Limitations

  • One strategy, one instrument, 472 trades over one strong uptrend.
  • %B is computed from the study's close-based bands, not V3.9's ohlc4 bands; a trade sitting on a bucket boundary can fall either side of it.
  • The filter sweep is applied to trades the strategy actually took. It cannot show what a differently-filtered strategy would have entered instead, so the retained-return column is an upper bound on the benefit, not a backtest.
  • Entry momentum is a proxy built from the same price series, so the ablation bounds the confound rather than eliminating it.
  • No result changes formal S1 logic, live risk, or an entry checklist without a separate adoption decision.
