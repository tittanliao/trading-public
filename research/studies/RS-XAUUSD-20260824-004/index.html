Twenty hypotheses on 18 years of daily gold, and the macro drivers the programme had data for and never opened

What was measured

Findings

Seven times the data did not turn any null into a finding

Twenty hypotheses on 4851 sessions, zero survivors, family permutation p=0.788. What changed is the bounds: the calendar claims that were arguably untested on 672 sessions now have resolution bounds around 0.11%, so 'Monday is worst' is closed rather than open. More data narrowed the question; it did not answer it differently.

A 71.5% win rate, and the 5.4-point reason it is not an edge

h18 fires 228 times and wins 71.49%. The days it is compared against win 59.94%, because the dollar series only covers 2021-2026 and gold rose through most of it. The first version of this study scored h18 against all 4851 sessions, produced a 54.56% baseline and a p of 0.032, and would have been published as a survivor. Restricting the comparison group to the condition's own era moved the baseline 5.38 points and the p-value to 0.351. This is the cleanest available demonstration that a win rate is a statement about the comparison group, not about the condition.

The real yield is the one macro channel worth more data

h17 — falling 10-year real yields precede a stronger month for gold — is the only macro condition whose sign holds in all three chronological windows. Its effect is +0.4808% against a resolution bound of 0.5277%: it misses by 0.048 percentage points. That is a specific instruction rather than a shrug. It is also the textbook mechanism, which makes it the one hypothesis here where a prior belief and the data point the same way.

The consensus rule fails both halves of its own test

Zero of eleven conditions stay above a 50% win rate in all five chronological blocks — every one has a losing period hidden inside a winning average. And win rate is not monotone in how many conditions agree. At five agreeing conditions the win rate is the highest in the table (59.78%) while the mean return is the only negative one (-0.38%). The 92 sessions behind that row make the exact figure unstable, but the direction is the opposite of what the rule predicts, and it is the fourth independent time this programme has separated win rate from expectancy.

Two design defects were found by inspecting a good-looking result

The first run rejected h18 for 'the sign does not hold across all three periods' when it had fired zero times in two of them — the split was taken over gold's 4851 sessions while the condition only existed for 1174. The same run compared macro conditions against years in which the macro series did not exist. Both were found by asking why a rejected hypothesis had a 71% win rate, not by a test failing. A screen that rejects for the wrong reason is more dangerous than one that does not fire.

How to read a null here

Every row carries a resolution bound: the smallest difference these two samples could have separated at roughly 80% power, computed as 2.8 × σ × √(1/n₁ + 1/n₂). An effect inside its bound means this sample cannot tell it from zero — not that it is zero. A null with a bound is reusable evidence; a null without one is a shrug.

The win rate is reported beside the baseline it has to be read against, never on its own. A rate above 50% is compatible with losing money, and the comparison column is usually where that becomes visible.

A 71% win rate that is not an edge

h18 fires 228 times and wins 71.49%. The sessions it is compared against win 59.94%, because the dollar series covers only 1174 of the 4851 sessions in this study and gold rose through most of that window. In an era where most days win, winning on 71% of them is close to average.

An earlier version of this study scored the same condition against all 4851 sessions. That produced a baseline of 54.56% and a bootstrap p of 0.032, and it would have been published as a survivor. Matching the comparison group to the condition's own era moved the baseline by 5.38 points and the p-value to 0.351.

The one thread worth more data

h17 — falling 10-year real yields precede a stronger month for gold — gives +0.4808% against a bound of 0.5277%. It misses by 0.048 percentage points, and it is the only macro condition whose sign holds in all three chronological windows. It is also the textbook mechanism. That combination makes it a data problem rather than an idea problem.

Does a 50% win rate mean an edge?

If consensus carries information, two things must hold: a condition's win rate stays above 50% in every block rather than only on average, and the win rate rises as more conditions agree. Either one failing means the pooled number was describing the period.

Limitations

  • Daily close-to-close only. Nothing here says anything about intraday entries, which is where both live strategies operate.
  • The macro series end 2026-06-18 while gold runs to 2026-08-21, so macro-conditioned hypotheses see two months less than the calendar ones.
  • Gold's 00:00Z daily stamp and the US macro close are different instants. The one-session lag removes the ambiguity at the cost of testing a slightly later entry than a live trader would get.
  • A wider resolution bound is not evidence of absence. Every null is reported with the smallest effect its sample could have separated.
