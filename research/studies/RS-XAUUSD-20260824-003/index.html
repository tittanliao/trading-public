What was measured

An objection was raised to repeated findings of no significance and proposed a different standard: a condition above 50% win rate is alpha, and several conditions agreeing is alpha. With the CFTC history downloaded, is the standard testable, and what does it produce?

Findings

The sample objection was fair and is now removed

The CFTC disaggregated history was downloaded with permission: 503 gold weeks from 2017, verified against their 31 screenshots at 279 of 279 field-values. Joined to the daily series it gives 274 usable weeks against the 31 that made the previous study unanswerable. The binding constraint is now the price history starting 2021-05, not the positioning data.

The Monday sweep gap is 29.8 points and it is circular

Weeks where Monday broke Friday's low finished Mon+Tue positive 33.6% of the time against 63.6% otherwise. But whether Monday broke the low is not knowable until Monday has closed, and the measured return includes Monday. Evaluating the condition at Monday's close and measuring Tuesday alone leaves a gap of 1.35 points. The 30-point version asks whether a down Monday was down.

A 50% threshold measures the period, not the condition

The Mon+Tue baseline win rate is 45.6% in the first half of the sample and 54.3% in the second — an 8.75-point shift as gold moved from range to trend. The same 50% line therefore means 'beats the market' in one period and 'loses to it' in the other. Of 182 comparable conditions, one cleared 50% in the first half while 80% of all conditions cleared it in the second, and the win rate of a condition in one half correlates with its win rate in the other at -0.077.

Consensus does what the claim said it would, and it costs money

Five of six positioning votes agreeing gives a 62.5% win rate against 48.8% — a genuine 13.7-point gap, and it survives a walk-forward reconstruction with every median computed from prior weeks only. It also earns -0.046% per week against +0.084%. It wins more often and smaller: +0.83% per win against +1.27%, -1.45% per loss against -1.05%, a payoff ratio of 0.55 against 1.22. Taking 11.7% of the opportunities captures -7.8% of the total return.

Win rate and expectancy are different quantities, demonstrated a third way

RS-XAUUSD-20260818-001 bought 88.6% win rate with a take-profit overlay and paid for it in return. RS-XAUUSD-20260823-002 bought 79.6% by refusing entries and paid the same way. This study buys 62.5% by requiring positioning consensus and pays again. Three different mechanisms, one arithmetic: a rule that raises win rate by being more selective or by cutting winners short has to be judged in return, and each time it has been, it lost.

The walk-forward version looks better, which is a reason for caution rather than confidence

Recomputing the votes with only prior data gives 63.2% against 49.3% on 19 weeks, and a positive mean-return gap of +0.157% where the full-sample version was negative. A restriction that improves a result is the opposite of the usual direction. Its permutation p values are 0.24 for the win-rate gap and 0.68 for the return gap, so the honest reading is that 19 weeks cannot separate the two selections.

A numpy boolean silently turned six votes into one

The walk-forward vote count was written as a chain of comparisons joined with +. Numpy booleans overload + as logical OR, so the sum collapsed to 0 or 1 and the vote distribution came back as {0: 1, 1: 243} with n=0 in the 5+ bucket. That result was visibly absurd and got caught. The same bug landing on a plausible number is the one that would not have been.

Limitations

  • 273 weeks joined to a daily series starting 2021-05. The CFTC history reaches 2017 and 503 weeks; the binding constraint is now price data, not positioning.
  • Returns are measured on daily closes. The intraday shape of the sweep hypothesis — break the low, then recover within the session — needs 30-minute data, which covers only 137 of these weeks.
  • The consensus votes were chosen by the executor, not optimised. A search over vote definitions would find better ones and would need its own correction.
  • No result changes formal S1 or S2 logic, live risk, or an entry checklist.
