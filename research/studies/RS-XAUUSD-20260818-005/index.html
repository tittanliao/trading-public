S1 V3.9 + S2 V3.2 — the two strategies as a portfolio, not as two strategies

S1 and S2 as one portfolio: correlation 0.234, and what that is worth.

HomeXAUUSDTXWhat Didn’t WorkGlossary

What was measured

After a bar-level alpha search found nothing tradeable, is there anything left in how the two existing strategies are combined — and can a genuinely uncorrelated third strategy be found for the same instrument, long or short?

0.234 monthly correlation pearson0.999 blend beats s2 p0.649 blend beats s1 p

Findings

The two strategies are much less redundant than they look

Monthly R returns correlate at 0.234 (Pearson) and 0.344 (Spearman) across 32 months, with both trading in 29 of them. Two long-only strategies on the same instrument were expected to be near-duplicates; they are not, and that is the whole basis for anything else here.

Blending is strongly better than running S2 alone

A 50/50 risk split lifts the annualised Sharpe from 2.327 to 3.882 and cuts the worst drawdown from 12.79R to 2.17R. Resampling months gives P(improvement > 0) = 0.999 for Sharpe and 0.99 for drawdown, with a 90% interval of [0.763, 2.377] on the Sharpe gain.

And it is NOT demonstrably better than running S1 alone

Against S1 the Sharpe gain is 0.218 with a 90% interval of [-0.753, 0.985] and P(>0) = 0.649. That interval crosses zero comfortably. Reporting only the S2 comparison would have turned a real but one-sided result into a recommendation the data does not carry.

The weight does not matter, which is the useful part

Sharpe runs from 3.707 to 3.958 across every S1 weight between 0.4 and 0.8. The in-sample optimum sits inside that plateau, so it carries no information. 50/50 is reported because it is the weight a person picks without looking, and it gives up almost nothing.

The Sharpe level is not a live expectation and the comparison is

Values near 3.9 do not survive real execution. These are Strategy Tester R multiples aggregated monthly over a market that rose 112%, with no slippage beyond the export's own. Every claim here is a ranking between allocations of the same backtest, never a forecast of what either will return.

Per year the blend leads twice out of three

The 50/50 blend has the best Sharpe of the three options in 2/3 years. In 2026 S1 alone is better, because S2 had a weak year — which is what a diversification result should look like rather than a uniform win.

The strategies are mostly alpha, so an uncorrelated third is not structurally blocked

Regressing monthly R on gold's monthly return gives S1 a beta of 0.444 and S2 0.27, with R-squared of 0.22 and 0.116. 79.7% of S1's return and 77.0% of S2's is alpha rather than being long gold, and in the 8 months gold fell both were still positive (4.06R and 1.507R). Had they been mostly beta, decorrelating would have required shorting gold and the search would have been hopeless. Caveat: this is backtest alpha, and the version history from V3.4 to V4.1 is itself a selection process.

A counter-trend fade is genuinely uncorrelated and genuinely unprofitable

Fading a two-bar move of 2.5 ATR, held 8 bars, is symmetric long and short and correlates 0.036 with the blend, -0.054 with S1 and 0.128 with S2 — exactly the property being looked for. Over 700 trades its own Sharpe is -0.046 and its total is -3.08R, so it lowers the blend's Sharpe at every weight tested (3.882 down to 2.911 at 30%). Two of four variants held their sign from train to valid; surviving that is not the same as being worth adding.

The structural mirror of S2 does not work as a short

S2's hammer definition was reverse-engineered from the export's own flags and each characteristic reflected — body, both wicks, close position and Bollinger position — giving 96 shooting-star signals against 212 hammers. Shorting them loses in every period and every exit rule tested, with win rates from 13% to 39%. A working long pattern does not become a working short by reflection.

The reusable output is the bar a new strategy has to clear

At a 30% risk allocation, a candidate needs a Sharpe of 0.8 to improve the blend if it is uncorrelated, 1.65 at correlation 0.25 and 2.44 at 0.50 — but only -0.14 at correlation −0.25, where a candidate helps while losing money slightly. Correlation is worth more than return, which argues for a different instrument rather than another rule on this one.

verdict
Genuinely uncorrelated and genuinely unprofitable. It clears the correlation half of the bar and fails the return half, so it lowers the blend's Sharpe at every weight. Surviving train-to-valid is not the same as being worth adding.

Limitations

  • Sharpe near 3.9 is not a live expectation. These are Strategy Tester R multiples over a market that rose 112% with no slippage beyond the export's own. The ranking between allocations is the result; the level is not.
  • 32 months is a small sample for a Sharpe comparison, which is why every claim here carries a bootstrap interval and why the S1 comparison is reported as unsupported rather than as a small positive.
  • Monthly aggregation assumes risk can be allocated between the two strategies at a stable ratio. With fixed-fractional sizing per trade that is what already happens; it is not a new mechanism.
  • Both strategies are long-only on one instrument in one regime. A correlation of 0.234 measured here need not hold in a falling market, and the diversification is between two expressions of the same directional bet.
  • No result changes formal S1 or S2 logic, live risk, or an active entry checklist.
