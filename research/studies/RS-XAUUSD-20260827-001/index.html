Position sizing for monthly gold-futures accumulation — when a backtest answers with the top of the tested range

Four monthly buys of gold futures held to March. The backtest clears every size tested because eighteen September cycles never met a large drawdown; the arithmetic stops well short.

What was measured

Findings

The question as usually asked answers with the top of the tested range

"The largest size with a zero margin-call rate" cannot be answered by eighteen September-to-March cycles whose worst trough against average cost is -14.18%, because the tightest margin trigger among the sizes tested is -18.96%. Every size clears, so the answer is whatever the range topped out at — 4.34x leverage here, and it would have been higher had higher been tested. That is a fact about eighteen draws, not about the calendar.

The arithmetic gives a different answer and it is the one that governs

Gold fell 29.44% between January and June 2026, the largest fall in the daily record. Margin call arrives at -28.17% at 3.1x leverage and -22.8% at 3.72x, so only 2.48x and below survive a repeat: its trigger is -36.24%. The fall happened outside the September build window, which is exactly why the backtest does not see it and cannot be used to rule it out.

Surviving margin and surviving the holder are different sizes

Two drawdowns are reported and they are not the same quantity. Trough against average cost is what margin tests, because maintenance compares equity to a requirement and equity is price minus cost. Peak-to-trough on the equity curve is larger and is what the position felt like: 37.09% at 2.48x leverage, and the worst case is a cycle that finished 20% up. It built near 3,900, rode gold to 5,597 in January and exited at 4,683, giving back a quarter of the account from its high on the way.

The seasonal premise is half backwards, and unresolvable in either direction

A December-to-March seasonal strength is often asserted. December and January are the strong pair; February and March are the two weakest months of the year, so holding to end-March captures January and then sits through both. But no month separates: 47 observations against a resolvable gap of about 21 percentage points, with the strongest month 11.7 points clear of the rest. The claim is neither supported nor refuted, and exit timing is reported as a parameter with its bound rather than as a recommendation.

Roll cost is excluded, and it is the largest omission

Carry on a 4,650 contract at prevailing rates puts a two-month roll near $36 an ounce while the front-month basis measurable here is $1 to $3 — and that basis is not the calendar spread, so it cannot settle the question. Two to three rolls sit between September and March. Against a mean cycle return of 6.2% of capital the difference between a token roll cost and a carry-implied one is most of the profit, so every figure here is optimistic and the leverage ceiling is an upper bound.

two kinds of drawdown

trough vs cost %
The trough measured against what the ounces cost. Margin is tested on this, because maintenance compares equity to a requirement and equity is price minus cost.
max equity drawdown %
Peak to trough on the equity curve. Larger, and large even in profitable cycles: 2025/09-2026/03 built at 3,900, rode gold to 5,597 in January and exited at 4,683 for +20%, giving back 9,809 dollars from its high on the way. It is what the position felt like, not what triggers a call.
why it matters
A size chosen on trough-vs-cost survives margin. A size chosen on equity drawdown survives the person holding it. They are different numbers and the second is the larger one.

why they differ
The eighteen September cycles never contained a drawdown past -15%, so every tested size clears them. The arithmetic asks a different question: which sizes survive a fall the size of the one gold delivered between January and June 2026, -29.44%. That fall happened outside the build window, which is why the backtest does not see it and why it cannot be used to rule it out.

Limitations

  • Roll cost is zero by instruction. Two to three rolls sit in each cycle and gold carries positive contango, so every net figure is optimistic.
  • Eighteen cycles. The worst of them is -14.18% from average cost while 6.5% of all seven-month windows in the same data fall past -19%.
  • XAUUSD spot stands in for a contract listed in 2025. Deferred-month liquidity is thinner than spot and the assumed fills are better than reachable ones.
  • Margins are held constant. CME raises them in exactly the conditions that would trigger a call, so a real breach arrives earlier than modelled.
  • No commission, no financing, no currency cost.
  • Monthly seasonality has 47 observations a month against a resolvable gap of roughly 21 percentage points. No month separates, in either direction.
