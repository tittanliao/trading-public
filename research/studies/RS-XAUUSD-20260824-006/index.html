What was measured

The programme's only surviving finding — S1 entries with %B above the upper band win 73.17% against a 55.93% baseline — has only ever been measured on 30-minute bars of one price feed. RS-XAUUSD-20260824-005 has just shown a 71% win rate invert when its measuring instrument was swapped. Does this one survive the same treatment?

Findings

The effect survives all four measurements

30-minute spot, hourly spot at matched bars, hourly spot at a matched clock window, and hourly COMEX futures — every variant clears its resolution bound, its label permutation and its bootstrap interval, on the same 422 trades. The effect is not an artefact of a bar size and not an artefact of one price feed. That is worth something: the same test destroyed a 71% win rate two studies ago.

The instrument test was weak, and the number says how weak

MGC and spot gold correlate 0.9998 on levels. The dollar test that inverted a finding swapped two indices correlated 0.8379. Passing an instrument test between two quotes of the same metal is a much weaker statement than failing one between two genuinely different constructions, and this study does not claim otherwise. A real instrument test for gold would need something that is not gold.

The effect is robust; the label a person acts on is not

30-minute and hourly spot — same instrument, same formula — assign the same %B zone to only 32.23% of trades. The 30-minute chart calls 71 entries above the upper band, the hourly chart calls 28, and they agree on 22 (Jaccard 0.2857). Even the two near-identical instruments at the same bar size share 23 of the 28 each selects. “Above the upper band” is not a property of the trade; it is a property of the chart that is open. This is invisible in a table of win rates, which is why per-trade agreement was measured instead of inferred from the headlines matching.

Selecting harder raised the win rate and gave up half the return

Variant B posts the best win rate in the study, 89.29%. It keeps 6.64% of trades and captures 27.6% of the return. Variant A wins 73.24% and captures 43.32%. Tightening the selection bought win-rate points and sold return. Fifth independent instance in this programme, and it turned up as a side effect of a test about something else.

Limitations

  • The common trade set is smaller than the original study's 472, so a variant failing here is not by itself proof the original was wrong — variant A is re-measured on the same subset precisely so the comparison is like for like.
  • MGC is a futures contract with its own roll and session; it is not spot gold quoted differently. That is the point of the test, and also its limit.
  • %B is computed on close with a sample standard deviation, the study convention. V3.9's Pine uses ohlc4 and a population stdev, so all four variants differ slightly from the strategy's own bands in the same way.
