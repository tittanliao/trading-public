Why the primary result is not family-corrected

It was written down by the previous study before this sample existed, so it is not a selection from this study's output and correcting it against this study's secondaries would penalise it for company it did not choose. The seven secondary hypotheses below are corrected as a family, and the two numbers are reported separately so nobody has to take that argument on trust.

Findings

The pre-registered hypothesis failed, and the reason is the interesting part

Falling 10-year real yields on 4443 sessions instead of 3196: effect +0.3803% against a 0.5648% bound. The single property that made this worth pursuing was that its sign held in all three chronological windows — the only macro condition in the predecessor that managed it. Adding 2008-2012 breaks exactly that: the middle window now runs -0.5421%. The stability was a feature of the sample, not of the relationship.

More data made the question harder, not easier

The prediction before the download was that the bound would fall to about 0.435%. It rose 7.0%, to 0.5648%. Two things: the universe grew 39% but the condition group did not (659 to 646, because an expanding percentile rank fires at whatever rate history dictates — 1.3% of 2018, 31.5% of 2020), and the bound is dominated by the smaller group, so the sample term moved only -2.7%. Meanwhile the added years are noisier: sigma rose 10.0%. A bigger sample narrows a bound only if what it adds is no noisier than what it had. 'Get more data' was this programme's standing answer to an underpowered null and it is not automatically the right one.

The 71% dollar result does not survive changing the dollar index

The predecessor's loudest number was a 71.49% win rate on dollar weakness, limited to 1174 sessions by an ICE DXY export starting in 2021. FRED's broad trade-weighted index starts in 2006 and would have tripled that, so it was first asked to reproduce the result on the 1174 sessions where both exist. Same window, same construction, opposite signs: ICE DXY +0.8773% and 70.98% win, broad TWI -0.2853% and 58.85% win against its own 62.85% baseline. The extension is refused — and a result that flips sign when a closely related measurement instrument is swapped in was never a finding. This is a cleaner refutation than the era-matched baseline was, and it came from a test run to enable the result rather than to attack it.

The regime version of the claim is also closed

'Gold does better when the real yield is negative' is the most commonly stated version of this whole family and had never been tested here as a regime rather than a quantile. m03 measures it directly and returns -1.1497% against a 0.4800% bound. Seven secondary hypotheses, zero survivors, family p=0.352.

FRED agrees with the existing exports where it can be checked

VIX and the 10-year breakeven are identical to the repository's TradingView copies — correlation 1.0000, median absolute difference 0.0000 — and the 10-year nominal agrees at 0.9998 on levels. That is worth recording as a provenance result in its own right: the manual exports are not a separate estimate of these series, they are the same numbers, so agreement between them can never be evidence of anything.

Limitations

  • Daily close-to-close only; nothing here bears on intraday entry timing.
  • The broad trade-weighted dollar index is not the ICE DXY. It is used only after a replication check on the overlapping window, and that check is reported whether or not it passes.
  • The 10-year real yield is a direct TIPS measurement from 2003, but gold's daily series starts 2008, so the extension is 2008-2026 rather than the full TIPS history.
  • A pre-registered hypothesis that fails on a larger sample is a stronger null than the same hypothesis failing on a smaller one. It is still a null.
